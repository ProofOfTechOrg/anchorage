The maintenance singleton can run notification dispatch as its own duty. Pass the closure `createNotificationDispatchTick()` returns as `createFlowsafeWorker({ notificationTick })`, with `maintenance.notificationIntervalMs`; `createFlowsafeMaintenanceDurableObject()` refuses one without the other. The duty runs in its own alarm invocation and logs a `notification-dispatch` line, or `notification-dispatch-error` when the tick throws. A host that ran notification dispatch inside its `scheduleTick` closure, as the advanced starter did, skipped delivery on every pass whose schedule work threw, and lost it when that invocation was terminated for exceeding its CPU limit. `MaintenanceDuty` gains `'notification'`, `runMaintenanceDuty('notification', env)` runs it, and `MaintenanceHealth` and the maintenance status gain `nextNotificationAt`, `lastNotificationAt`, `lastNotificationAttemptAt` and `lastNotificationError` while the duty is configured. Upgrade the fleet control plane to `@proofoftech/fleet-control` 0.6.0 before a deployment moves notifications into this duty: an earlier fleet control reads no notification fields, so a failing notification duty, which a failing schedule tick used to surface, raises no finding. `scheduleTick` and `tickIntervalMs` keep their meaning, so a host that still composes notifications into its schedule tick works unchanged; an exhaustive `switch` over `MaintenanceDuty` needs a case for the new member.
